# II. [Art]

<a id="s-0950"></a>
Nature, in its blind and mechanical purposiveness, admittedly represents to me an original identity of the conscious and unconscious activities, but [for all that,] it does not present this identity to me as one whose ultimate ground resides <em>in the self itself.</em> The transcendental philosopher assuredly recognizes that the principle of this [harmony] is that ultimate in ourselves[^n258-1] which already undergoes division in the primary act of selfconsciousness, and on which the whole of consciousness, with all its determinations, is founded; but the <em>self itself</em> is not aware of this. Now the aim of <em>our whole science</em> was in fact <em>precisely</em> this, of explaining how the ultimate ground of the harmony between subjective and objective becomes an object to the <em>self itself.</em>

<a id="s-0951"></a>
An intuition must therefore be exhibitable in the intelligence itself, whereby in <em>one and the same</em> appearance the self is at once conscious and unconscious <em>for itself,</em> and it is by means of such an intuition that we first bring forth the intelligence, as it were, entirely out of itself; by such an intuition, therefore, that we also first resolve the entire [the supreme] problem of transcendental philosophy (that of explaining the congruence between subjective and objective).

<a id="s-0952"></a>
By the first specification, namely that conscious and unconscious activity become objective <em>in one and the same intuition,</em> this intuition is distinguished from that which we were able to deduce[^n259-1] in practical philosophy, where the intelligence was conscious only for inner intuition, but for outer remained unconscious.

<a id="s-0953"></a>
By the second specification, namely that in one and the same intuition the self become simultaneously conscious <em>for itself,</em> and unconscious, the intuition here postulated is distinguished from that which we have in the case of natural products, where we certainly recognize this identity, but not as an identity whose principle lies in the self itself. Every organism is a monogram[^n259-2] of that original identity, but in order to recognize itself in that reflected image, the self must already have recognized itself directly in the identity in question.

<a id="s-0954"></a>
We have only to analyze the features of this intuition we have now deduced, in order to discover the intuition itself; and, to judge beforehand, it can be no other than the <em>intuition of art.</em>
