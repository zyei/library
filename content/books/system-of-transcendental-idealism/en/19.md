# Part Three: System of Theoretical Philosophy according to the Principles of Transcendental Idealism

<a id="s-0265"></a>
## <em>Introductory</em>

<a id="s-0266"></a>
1\. The self-consciousness we start from is <em>an absolute act,</em> and by this one act is posited, not only the self itself, with all its determinations, but also, as is sufficiently evident from the preceding part, everything else as well that is posited at all for the self. Our first concern in theoretical philosophy will therefore be the deduction of this absolute act.

<a id="s-0267"></a>
But in order to discover the full content of this act we are obliged to take it apart and split it up, as it were, into a number of individual acts. These latter will be <em>mediating elements</em> in that one absolute synthesis.

<a id="s-0268"></a>
From these individual acts, taken all together, we can, as it were, have <em>successively</em> presented to our eyes what is posited simultaneously and at once in the one absolute synthesis in which they are all incorporated.

<a id="s-0269"></a>
The procedure of this deduction is as follows:

<a id="s-0270"></a>
The act of self-consciousness is ideal and real, simultaneously and throughout. By means of it, what is posited as real is also immediately posited as ideal, and what is posited as ideal is likewise posited as real. This <em>thoroughgoing</em> identity of ideal and real positedness in the act of self-consciousness can only be presented in philosophy as arising in succession. This takes place in the following manner.

<a id="s-0271"></a>
The concept we start from is that of the self, that is, of the subject-object, to which we elevate ourselves through absolute freedom. Through this act there is now, <em>for us</em> who philosophize, something posited in the self <em>qua object,</em> but hence not yet posited therein <em>qua subject</em> (for the <em>self as such,</em> what is posited as real is in one and the same act also posited as ideal); our enquiry will therefore have to go on until what is posited for us in the self <em>qua</em> object is also posited for us in the self <em>qua</em> subject, that is, until for us the consciousness of our object coincides with our own consciousness, and thus until the self itself has for us arrived at the point from which we started.

<a id="s-0272"></a>
This procedure is necessarily carried out by means of our object, and by our endeavor, since subject and object—which are absolutely united in the absolute act of self-consciousness—must be constantly kept distinct for purposes of philosophizing, that is, in order to allow this unification to take place before our eyes.

<a id="s-0273"></a>
2\. In accordance with the foregoing, the enquiry will divide into two parts. First we shall derive the absolute synthesis contained in the act of self-consciousness, and afterwards must seek out the mediating elements of the synthesis in question.
