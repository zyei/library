###### C. Repulsion and Attraction

That the one repels itself from itself—this is **repulsion**. It is that element of *distinction* between the ones which keeps them separate. Each one excludes the other ones from it. It is only by virtue of the fact that it excludes the others that it retains its self-identity and continues to be a one. Each one only maintains itself as one by cutting itself off from the other ones, by enforcing the boundary between them.

But the many are one the same as another. Each is one. Hence they are one and the same. All are identical. This bond of *identity* is the opposite of that element of distinction and mutual exclusion which has been called repulsion. Hence this relation of identity is called **attraction**. We must not be misled into supposing that Hegel is here attempting to deduce the physical forces of attraction and repulsion. The names used are indeed physical metaphors. But they are purely figurative. Repulsion merely means that the many ones exclude each other. Attraction merely means that they are at the same time identical. Repulsion is the mutual exclusion of the ones by each other, whereby they emphasize their *difference*. Attraction is their mutual inclusion and *identity*.
