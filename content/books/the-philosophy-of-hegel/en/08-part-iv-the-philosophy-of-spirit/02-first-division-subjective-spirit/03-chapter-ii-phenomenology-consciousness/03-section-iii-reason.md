##### Section III. Reason

<a id="p-491"></a>
**491.** The ego now recognizes the independence of other egos. But since the other ego is for my self-consciousness another self-consciousness, it is therefore simply and solely myself. Ego has ego for its object. In contemplating the other it contemplates only itself. There are, therefore, two factors present here. Firstly, the object is an independent other. Secondly, the object is only myself, *i.e.* is not an independent other. The subject admits the distinction between itself and its object, but yet asserts that this distinction is no distinction, that the distinction is *within* itself. The subject has the object over against it but yet overlaps and overreaches the object and keeps it within itself. This is the point of view of **reason** (*Vernunft*). For reason is the principle which while admitting the distinction sees also the underlying identity. It is the principle of the identity of opposites. The object is now both distinct from the subject and yet identical with it.

It will be observed that reason, as the third member of the triad,—consciousness, self-consciousness, reason,—is the unity of the other two. The position of consciousness is that the object is independent, is distinct from the subject. The position of self-consciousness is that the object is identical with the subject. Reason combines these two abstract positions in a synthesis. The object is now both distinct from the subject and identical with it. It is identity in difference.
